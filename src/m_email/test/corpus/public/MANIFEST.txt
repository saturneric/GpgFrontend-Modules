Public MIME corpus for the e-mail module
========================================

Messages taken verbatim from third-party test suites. Where they came from,
under what licence, and with what hashes: ../PROVENANCE.md. Nothing here was
edited on the way in; nothing here is GpgFrontend's to define.

What these are for
------------------
The golden bucket is a specification: every message there is well-formed
because we wrote it that way. That makes it a good spec and a poor adversary.
These are the messages real mail actually arrives as -- sloppy boundaries,
missing separators, legacy charsets, bare LF, text where a header should be.

Rules
-----
* No byte-for-byte assertions. We did not author these bytes and do not
  promise to reproduce them. The exceptions are the paths that explicitly
  promise byte fidelity: RawHeaderBlock() and decoded attachment payloads.
* Line endings are whatever upstream shipped, mostly bare LF. Nothing here may
  assume CRLF; anything that needs CRLF belongs in golden/.
* Parsing is NOT required to succeed. Each message declares one of three
  outcomes in EMailPublicCorpusTest.cpp, and the test enforces that one:
      ACCEPT                            parsed, and the structure is the one
                                        the sender plainly meant
      ACCEPT_WITH_DEGRADED_STRUCTURE    parsed, but the damage cost us
                                        structure; safe and useful, not
                                        faithful
      REJECT                            refused outright -- never a crash, a
                                        hang, or a half-built tree returned as
                                        if it were good
  Alongside the outcome, every message must parse deterministically (same
  bytes, same tree, twice) and stay inside EMailParseLimits.
* One primary property per file. Product policy -- which security state the UI
  shows, which alternative is preferred -- is a contract we own, so it is
  pinned in golden/, not here.
* The differential test against the pre-tree walker runs over golden/ only.
  Malformed input legitimately diverges between two implementations; making
  them agree on garbage is not a goal.

Messages
--------
p01-boundary-name-clash.eml            ACCEPT
    Nested multipart where the child boundary is the parent boundary plus
    "-1", and the boundary value itself starts with "--". A parser that treats
    "line starts with the parent delimiter" as a match closes the inner
    multipart early and reparents the PDF attachment. Pins: the alternative
    keeps its two children and the attachment stays a child of the root.

p02-ending-boundaries.eml              ACCEPT_WITH_DEGRADED_STRUCTURE
    Trailing text on the delimiter lines ("--boundary This should be
    ignored"). RFC 2046 allows only transport padding there, so vmime finds no
    delimiter and the whole body becomes preamble: zero parts.
    mime4j, whose message this is, recovers the part instead. We do not, and
    that is deliberate -- recovering means guessing which bytes the sender
    meant to delimit, and an ambiguously framed message is exactly the one
    where a wrong guess decides what appears to be signed. Pins: no part is
    invented from the preamble.

p03-boundary-text-in-body.eml          ACCEPT
    The string "--boundary" appears inside a body, mid-sentence. Pins: it does
    not split the part, and both lines survive in the body.

p04-no-header-separator.eml            ACCEPT_WITH_DEGRADED_STRUCTURE
    A bare non-header line sits in the middle of the header block. Pins: the
    Subject before the damage is still parsed and the body does not vanish.

p05-base64-rfc822-bare-lf.eml          ACCEPT
    A message/rfc822 that is itself base64-encoded, and whose inner message
    uses bare LF throughout. Two decoding layers before any structure is
    visible. Pins: the inner payload decodes.

p06-complex-multipart.eml              ACCEPT
    A realistic four-part message with binary attachments -- the everyday
    complex case, NOT a size test. Real size and depth stress is generated in
    the test at run time rather than checked in as a blob. Pins: attachment
    payloads decode non-empty.

p07-cte-matrix.eml                     ACCEPT
    7bit, quoted-printable, base64, base64-with-trailing-newline, and a fifth
    part with no Content-Transfer-Encoding header at all, across us-ascii and
    iso-8859-1. Pins: all five decode, the CTE-less part included.

p08-nested-charsets.eml                ACCEPT
    Five root children, one of them a nested multipart/mixed, spanning
    us-ascii, iso-8859-1, iso-8859-2, iso-8859-3 and koi8-r. Every body is
    empty. Pins: empty bodies are still parts, so the nesting survives.

p09-duplicate-attachment-names.eml     ACCEPT
    Two attachments both claiming name="msg.txt", neither with a
    Content-Disposition. Pins: UniqueAttachmentFileNames() separates them, and
    does so the same way every time.

p10-iso2022jp-encoded-subject.eml      ACCEPT
    RFC 2047 B-encoded Subject split across two adjacent encoded-words in
    ISO-2022-JP. Pins: no encoded-word syntax survives into the decoded value,
    and the two words are joined before decoding rather than separately.

p11-empty-multipart.eml                ACCEPT
    A multipart/alternative whose own boundary never appears, followed by a
    text/plain at the PARENT's level. A parser that keeps consuming until it
    finds the child boundary adopts that sibling. Pins: it does not -- which
    under a multipart/signed is the difference between signed and unsigned.

p12-preamble-epilogue.eml              ACCEPT
    Text before the first boundary and after the closing one. Pins: neither
    becomes a part or leaks into one.

Adding to this corpus
---------------------
Only when a candidate covers a parser behaviour nothing here reaches. A second
message with the same shape costs run time and review attention and buys
nothing. Effort is better spent on the golden OpenPGP fixtures, which define
security semantics no public corpus can define for us.
